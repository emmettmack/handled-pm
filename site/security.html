Security and trust

What is in place, and what is not.

You are trusting this with your tenants' names, phone numbers, photos and addresses. Here is the plain version of how it is handled, including the things I have not done yet.

In place today

Your own database

Every account's data lives in its own database file. One customer's data is never read while serving another's.

You approve what is sent

Every message to a vendor or owner is a draft until you approve it. The assistant never promises money, fault or arrival times.

Backups

Daily backups of every account, kept for a week, and downloadable so a copy can live somewhere other than the server.

Signed requests

Texts from Twilio are accepted only if they carry a valid signature. Photos are served through short-lived signed links, not public addresses.

Texting rules

STOP, START and HELP are honored. People who opt out are never texted. Consent wording is on a public page.

No message contents in admin tools

The admin tools I use show usage and system health, not your tenants' messages, names or phone numbers.

The companies involved

  • Twilio sends and receives text messages.
  • Anthropic provides the AI that reads messages and photos.
  • Clerk handles sign-in.
  • Render hosts the app and its data.

Details on what each one receives are in the privacy policy.

Not in place yet

  • Formal certification. No SOC 2 or similar audit. For a product this size, I would rather say so than imply it.
  • Field-level encryption of tenant details inside the database. Data is protected in transit and by the host's disk, but this extra layer is planned.
  • Self-serve export and deletion. Today I do both for you on request.
  • Team roles. Each account is one person's login for now.

If something goes wrong

If I find that tenant data was exposed, I will tell the affected customers directly and promptly. To report a problem or ask a question, email efmack@usc.edu.

This page describes the product as it is today and will change as it does.
